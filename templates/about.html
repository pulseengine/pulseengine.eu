Three claims, three things shipping, two unknowns

3 claims we're betting on

  1. AI agents collapse the cost of writing proof annotations to near-zero — formal verification stops being elite.
  2. Oracle-gated agent pipelines are the missing half of spec-driven development. Read the post.
  3. Variant pruning makes MC/DC for AI-authored Rust strictly easier than MC/DC for C, once all five layers are counted. The argument.

3 things shipping today

  1. witness — MC/DC on WebAssembly. v0.10. Coverage reports, DSSE-signed attestations, in-toto provenance.
  2. gale — formally-verified Rust port of Zephyr RTOS primitives. 805 Verus properties · 39/39 modules · Rocq, Lean, Kani harnesses.
  3. rivet — schema-driven SDLC traceability. Schemas for ISO 26262, DO-178C, IEC 61508, ASPICE, STPA, EU AI Act. rivet validate on every commit.

2 things we don't know yet

  1. Whether checkable-certificate emitters (Z3, Verus, Kani) can collapse DO-330 qualification cost from "qualify the prover" to "qualify a small checker." In progress.
  2. Whether AI-velocity verification dossiers survive a real authority audit. We have not been audited.

Get involved

Open source. Everything happens in public on the GitHub org — issues, pull requests, discussions, releases. Posts and notes on the blog (RSS at /atom.xml). Every commit is on GitHub, every release is signed, every proof is rerunnable.

Found something interesting, broken, or worth a deeper post? Open an issue on the relevant repo. Working in a regulated domain and curious how the chain maps to your dossier? The six-domain credit matrix is the entry point — and the comment thread on the relevant tracking issue is where conversations happen in the open.

Specific standard? See what PulseEngine offers each — DO-178C+DO-333 · ISO 26262 · IEC 61508-3 · EN 50128 · IEC 62304 · ECSS-Q-ST-80C — in the credit matrix. Per-domain qualification material is being assembled in the open as the work proceeds.
